## Color — what makes a piece land

*(tunnel_v1)* Color was the thing Nick liked most about the piece, and he named all four parts of it:

- **Muted, not neon.** Rich but dusty: psychedelic, not rave. Agrees with the palette's 30–40% desaturation rule.
- **Against black.** Color glows out of dark gaps between the forms; the black stays in the frame even when the frame is full.
- **The mix.** Mauve, copper/amber and teal-slate together, with bone highlights. A warm, cool and purple chord, not one hue family.
- **The shift.** Iridescence: color changes across each surface as the angle changes, so the color moves even when the hues don't.

## Light — a center to move toward

*(tunnel_v1)* A bright white light at the vanishing point, pulling everything toward it. Nick likes it; it belongs in **portal pieces** (tunnels, descents, anything you move *into*), not as a default for every visual.

## Density — overload is allowed

*(tunnel_v1)* A frame packed edge to edge is fine **anytime**, not only at peaks. Overload is part of the look in general.

## Symmetry — it's about order

*(tunnel_v1)* Four-way mirror symmetry is part of why this felt like Wobar, and what it's doing is **order**: it makes chaos look designed. Nick doesn't read it as a face or a mandala.

## Form — open

*(tunnel_v1)* No preference between soft (pills, capsules) and hard-edged geometry. The forms are a surface for the color.

## Pulls against WOBAR_BRAND

Noted here, not resolved. WOBAR_BRAND stays as written until Nick decides.

- **Gloss.** Texture & Finish says *matte, not glossy; avoid metallic or reflective, except mirror moments.* tunnel_v1's iridescence depends on a glossy surface, and the color it produced is the part Nick liked most.
- **Pure white.** The palette says highlights are *never pure white.* tunnel_v1's center light is pure white, and Nick wants it kept for portal pieces.
