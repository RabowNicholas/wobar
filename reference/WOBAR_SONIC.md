## The Wobar Sound
Psychedelic because the sound always points inward. Experimental because the three versions decide what's needed — not genre convention. Bass is the constant vehicle because it reaches the body before the mind catches up.

The only question that determines whether something is Wobar: **does it point inward.**

Dark, wonky 140 is the dialect, not the test. Most Wobar music lives at 140 — sub-forward, halftime, sound-design-dense. The membership question never changes. Structurally proven live: Acts 1–4 can live inside the 140 band across its registers; Act 5 settles below it — pure integration, melting-type moments.

Heavy bass that serves the journey. Sometimes confrontational and disorienting. Sometimes emotional and cathartic. Always psychedelic: organic sound design, spatial movement, textures that evolve and breathe. Groove and danceability matter. The dance floor stays moving while the work happens.

## Genre Positioning

**Corrected 2026-07-27.** This section previously named "Deep Psychedelic Dubstep" as the home register, which is the same flattening the comp table below fixes — it describes registers 1/2/6 and skips the 45% of runtime that is registers 3 and 4.

- **Primary — dark / wonky 140.** Sub-forward, halftime, sound-design-dense, gritty. The centre of mass (29% alone, 45% with the rap-flip register). This is what "most Wobar music sounds like."
- **Introduction layer — what a promoter or first-contact listener is told.** Lead with **"dark, wonky 140."** *"Deep dubstep"* is a **register-1/2 descriptor and should not be the default first-contact framing** — it was, and it is what put psy-bass comps in front of wonky-140 labels. Register-match the descriptor to the recipient (swap table in [[reference/WOBAR_COPY]] BIO TEMPLATES).
- **The full arc, in order:** deep/hypnotic → deep-dubstep hinge → **dark/wonky 140** → rap-flip → dub pivot → psy-bass outro. Range beyond it when the act requires: drum & bass (Act 4 ceiling), midtempo/downtempo (Act 5 floor).
- **Never use "EDM" as a genre descriptor for Wobar.**

## The Three Versions in Sound

**The Wounded Version in Sound**
Heavy, raw. Matches the state before asking it to move. Hypnotic grooves that hold rather than push. Dissonant textures that don't apologize. Angry frequencies that validate before they transform.

**The Future Version in Sound**
Melodic weight after heaviness. Philosophical samples as notes left by someone who already knows the way. West coast hip hop influence — conscious, joyful, grounded. Heavy AND pretty simultaneously.

**The Present Version in Sound**
The container — the sound design decisions, the structural arc, the sequencing. Doesn't resolve the tension between wounded and future. Makes space for both.

## Anti-References
- **Excision/Subtronics** — Too aggressive, no therapeutic arc
- **Skrillex/brostep** — Mid-range focused, lacks sub foundation
- **Pure psytrance** — Wrong approach entirely
- **"Healing" music without edge** — Wobar has teeth, not just vibes

> **⚠ Anti-reference ≠ anti-label. Reconciled 2026-07-27.**
> These are **sonic** calls about what Wobar's music does not sound like. They are **not** a target list for label submissions, and reading them that way would cost two of the better doors found in the 2026-07 research:
> - **Subsidia is Excision's label** — and it is the highest-appearance door in the whole sweep (**8** across ISEEU, STVSH ×2, Gunpoint ×4). Its *Dusk* / *Night* series carry STVSH, OkayJake, Gunpoint, Wiley, XotiX at 20–29 artists per volume. It is a broad VA machine, not a tearout label.
> - **Cyclops Recordings is Subtronics'** — separately dropped as a **founder vehicle** (~8/10 of its top sellers are Subtronics), which is the real reason, not the anti-reference.
>
> **The rule:** don't *sound* like Excision; that says nothing about whether his label will release a dark/wonky 140 record by someone else. Judge doors on roster, founder share and register fit ([[working/RESEARCH_LABEL_LANDSCAPE]]), never on the label owner's own production style.
