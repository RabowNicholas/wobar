# LANDSCAPE / EMPTY-WORLD FOOTAGE SOURCES

Ask was: highest-quality places that exist for **nature footage — landscape/empty-world, not wildlife** — and their prices. This is the map, not a curation.

---

## Tier 1 — Curated cinematic agencies (highest per-shot craft, single-use licensing)

**Filmsupply** — the standout. Represents an elite roster of story-driven cinematographers (mountains, deserts, storms, oceans, auroras) rather than running as an open marketplace. Single-use license, scaled by usage:
- $99/clip — small internal use
- up to $5k+/clip — TV, film, advertising
[filmsupply.com/pricing](https://www.filmsupply.com/pricing)

## Tier 2 — Individual landscape/timelapse artists (license direct, no storefront)

No public rate cards — email direct.
- **Mike Olbinski** — storms, supercells, lightning. Licenses via his own site and through Filmsupply. [mikeolbinski.com/storms/licensing](https://www.mikeolbinski.com/storms/licensing/)
- **Tom Lowe / TimeScapes** — night sky, stars, deserts.
- **Dustin Farrell / Sunchaser Pictures** — landscape timelapse.
- **TSO Photography / Terje Sørgjerd** — auroras, volcanoes, mountains.

## Tier 3 — Aerial/drone-specific libraries

- **Aerial Earth** — sweeping landscapes, mountain vistas, licensing on request. [aerialearth.io](https://aerialearth.io/pages/license-footage)
- **DroneVideos.com** — flat-rate 4K clips, $10–$40/clip.
- **Drone Footage 4K** — one-time fee for lifetime access to full library.
- General market range for drone landscape licensing: $50–500 non-exclusive, $200–2,000 exclusive.

## Tier 5 — Free landscape/nature footage

- **Pixabay** — 50,000+ nature/landscape clips, free.
- **Mixkit, Coverr, Vecteezy, Pexels** — free landscape-tagged libraries. Coverr now mixes in AI-generated clips — check per-clip.

## Tier 6 — Public domain / archival, genuinely empty-world

- **NASA** — orbital Earth footage, storms from space. Free.
- **NOAA** — ocean, weather, atmospheric footage. Free.
- **USGS / National Park Service** — landscape and geological footage. Free.
- **archive.org** — mixed quality; includes old expedition/geological survey film with no people or wildlife. Also the Prelinger Archives collection.
- **Library of Congress National Screening Room, BFI Player (free tier)** — older archival film, variable transfer quality.

---

## Adjacent — wildlife-focused houses (surveyed first, less relevant to the "empty world" brief but noted for completeness)

- **Getty Images** (incl. BBC Motion Gallery / BBC Natural History Unit, now distributed exclusively through Getty) — top of market for wildlife cinematography specifically. Royalty-free $125–$500/clip (HD/4K flat ~$499); Rights-Ready is quote-based.
- **NatureFootage.com** — largest niche HD/4K+ collection for wildlife/oceans/underwater specifically. Royalty-free from $49, Rights-Managed from $60.
- **Off the Fence, Terra Mater Studios, Java Films** — documentary/wildlife licensing houses, quote-only.

---

## Rough ceiling-to-floor read

Filmsupply + the named individual timelapse artists (Olbinski, Lowe, Farrell, Sørgjerd) sit above even Getty for this specific category — Getty's landscape content is a subset of a wildlife-and-everything catalog, not a specialty. Below that: Artgrid is the best subscription option for landscape specifically. NASA/NOAA are the best free option if orbital/atmospheric shots fit the need.
