# IG COMP SWEEP — SAMPLE FRAME

## Why this exists
The first cohort (RESEARCH_IG_COMPS v1.0, n=4) claimed neutral-frame sampling but was actually drawn on **whose Instagram handle was resolvable by web search** — which correlates with press coverage, management and career infrastructure. That is why all four over-indexed and no flatliner appeared. This document rebuilds the population properly, before any handle is looked up.

## Frame definition
**Every artist credited on a release by Jadū Dala or Wubaholics, as listed on Beatport, pulled 2026-07-26.**
- Jadū Dala — most recent 150 releases (page 1 of 2; sorted newest→oldest)
- Wubaholics — complete catalogue, 121 releases

Credited = lead artist, collaborator, or remixer. No filtering on perceived size, activity, or whether the artist "looks successful."

**Rule for the sweep that follows:** resolve handles for the whole frame (or a recency-bounded subset of it), record follower count for every one, bucket by tier, then analyse the 1K–20K band. Tier is a definition of the population, not a selection on outcome. **Artists whose handle cannot be resolved get logged as unresolved and counted** — so the residual bias stays visible instead of disappearing.

## Frame size and the reduction problem

**~230 unique artists.** Too many to sweep by hand, so the frame needs cutting — and the cut has to stay neutral. Three options:

| Option | Rule | Resulting n | Neutral? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Recency window (recommended)** | Every artist credited on the **newest 40 releases per label** | ~55–65 | ✅ Time-based, no outcome correlation, and biases toward *currently active* artists — which is what the comp is actually about |
| **B — Lead artists only** | Drop features and remixers, keep primary credits across the full frame | ~150 | ✅ Neutral, but still too large |
| **C — Full frame** | All ~230 | 230 | ✅ Most defensible, impractical by hand |

**A + B combined** — lead artists on the newest 40 releases per label — lands around **40–50 names**, which is sweepable and still contains whoever the roster actually contains, working or not.

## Handle resolution — the part that broke last time
Do **not** resolve handles by web search. Instead pull them from **`@jadudala` and `@wubaholics` own Instagram grids** — labels tag every artist in release posts, regardless of whether that artist has press, management, or any following at all. That is the source that decouples handle-discoverability from career success.

Unresolvable handles get logged with a count, not silently dropped.

## What the sweep will record, per artist
followers · post count · 10 most-recent Reel view counts · median · top · **views ÷ followers** · dominant format tag · face vs faceless · recurring series present (y/n) · handle source (label tag / search / guess) · resolution status

## The two questions it must answer
1. Does **reach efficiency** track format, or only account size?
2. Do artists in the 1K–20K band running a **recurring series** beat one-off posters — the serialization thesis, tested at Wobar's scale rather than at 45K?

Jadū Dala and Wubaholics were chosen because they were the most **visible in Beatport chart data** — which means they were selected partly on their artists' success. Same bias as cohort v1, one level up.

Pulling four more rosters exposed a second problem: **the two-label frame was lane-incomplete, not just tier-incomplete.** It covered the dark-140 middle of Nick's set and was blind to the opening.
