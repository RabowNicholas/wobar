# Burner account strategy (2026-11-17)

Companion to CONTENT_TEST_PLAN_2026-11-17.md. Evidence: working/format_trends_finder_2026-11-17/TEMPLATES.md. Decisions are Nick's, made in session.

## Purpose
Not about followers. Burners are a testing ground and an ecosystem: find formats that travel, using Nick's own music only, at up to 5 posts a day per account. Scored on views only; conversion is tested later.

## Why it can work (evidence)
- On TikTok, the best template pages get 1.2 to 11 views per follower, against about 0.37 for artist accounts. Templates break the follower ceiling.
- Most template pages are lottery tickets: of 37 read, only 12 had 10+ of their last 12 posts over 10k; many have one 5M to 50M pinned hit and recent posts in the hundreds. The consistent ones post on rhythm and give the viewer a reason to come back (a utility frame: "Designed For Edits", "Artist ID", "headphones on").
- The real failure rate is higher than seen: search only surfaces pages that already hit. Expect some burners to fail; judge each over its first 30 posts.

## The post unit
**b-roll + one text line + one section of the song.** The track is the constant; the visual system is the template. Every post uses a new clip (no reuse), or TikTok's repetition filters choke the account.

## Accounts (launch with two)
**A. The void (rendered).** Liminal spaces, PS1 / low-poly worlds, megastructures (Blame!-style), all made in Blender. No rights risk, endless variation, closest to the brand; could later feed the main page. Found footage (deep sea, space, night tunnels; mostly public domain or Nick's own) runs as a variable inside A, tagged per post. Spin it off only if it clearly beats renders.

**B. Quiet anime frames.** Serial Experiments Lain, Evangelion, Berserk, Blame!, Akira / Ghost in the Shell. Atmospheric frames only: empty skies, silhouettes, Lain's wires, Eva's empty train car. Not fight scenes (fights + drop is the figurative-cheese vector). Separate account because anime edit viewers are their own community and it isolates the rights risk (copyrighted footage can draw takedowns or strikes; the audio is Nick's, the visuals are the risk).

**Why two, not three:** found footage pulls the same audience as A, and 3 accounts at 5 a day is 15 posts a day. Rhythm is the biggest finding; two accounts on schedule beat three that slip.

### Why these fit WOBAR
- **Liminal spaces** are the void with a floor: an empty space plus one focal element (the vector-void rule). A threshold ("in-between place") matches the Portal framework. Dread with no explanation is "shown, never explained". Restraint is built in: no faces, muted light, stillness.
- **The right dark anime** is about interior worlds: the self, the mirror, dissolution (Evangelion), the wired world and identity (Lain, closest of all: it already looks like wireframe, signal and void), descent (Berserk). A person facing something vast and unexplained is the WOBAR stance.

### Other niche ideas (parked)
- Same thread (vast space, lone figure, unexplained rules): FromSoftware landscapes (Bloodborne, Dark Souls, Elden Ring; not boss fights), deep sea / bioluminescence (NOAA, largely public domain), space solitude (lone astronaut, slow drift), night tunnels and empty highways, brutalist architecture in fog.
- Sports with a built-in drop moment (MMA / boxing knockouts, skateboarding, snowboard / freeride, motocross, BMX, F1 night onboards) and edit niches from the TikTok read (night JDM / drift, gym / phonk crossover, horror / 3D horror). Off the brand thread; parked.
- Not for WOBAR: anime fight edits, Kanye / military / car fandom edits. Highest views per follower in the read (5 to 11) but figurative fandom content.

## Test axes
| # | Axis | Options |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audio treatment | Original, slowed + reverb, sped up, bass boosted, edit-audio cut (clean 15 to 20s loop for editors). Multiplies the catalogue: one track becomes four sounds, which also eases the repetition problem. Highest-leverage new axis. |
| 2 | Song section | Drop, build into drop, hook / quiet moment, outro |
| 3 | Text type | Relatable specific situation, useful cue ("headphones on", "wait for the second drop"), in-world line, question, no text (control) |
| 4 | First second | Silence then hit, mid-drop start, text first, visual first |
| 5 | Sync style | One long shot, cuts on every beat, single impact cut on the drop |
| 6 | Loop design | Seamless loop vs hard ending (rewatches count as views) |
| 7 | Length | 7 to 10s, 15s, 25 to 30s |
| 8 | Grade / frame | B&W, one colour, natural; full frame vs letterbox |
| 9 | Series framing | Numbered, recurring title, none (weak evidence) |
| 10 | Caption / sound name | Sound named for reuse ("[track] (edit audio)"), hashtags, plain |
| 11 | Posting time / cadence | Spread vs clustered; check last |

## Phases (one variable set at a time; 11 at once needs hundreds of posts)
- **Phase 1 (weeks 1 to 2):** imagery (per account) x audio treatment x song section. Hold text, sync, length, grade fixed per account.
- **Phase 2:** keep the winners; test text type and first second.
- **Phase 3:** fine-tune loop, length, grade, series framing.

## Rules
- One template per account; never cross-boost between accounts (no coordinated likes, comments or reposts).
- New clip every post; text and section rotation help each post read as original.
- Datamosh, if used here, follows the same rule as the main page: drop only, 1 to 3 seconds.
- Results are read together (Nick + Claude); no automatic kill rules.
