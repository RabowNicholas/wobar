# Step 2: Reach read
For each account, read the last 12 non-pinned Reels / TikToks from the grid: views, plus followers.
Compute for each account:
- **Hit count:** how many of the 12 cleared a hit threshold (default 10k views; choose one that splits my tier sensibly and tell me).
- **Median views** of the 12.
- **Views per follower** (median views / followers). Artists typically sit well under 1; anything above 1 means posts are reaching non-followers.
- **Breakouts:** posts at 2x or more of that account's own median.
Rank by hit count, then median. **Checkpoint 2:** show me the top of the ranking and the distribution.

# Step 3: Content read (the part that matters)
For the top accounts (start with about 30, more if time allows), open the grid and read every cover by eye, plus the post pages for captions, audio and collab info. For each post record: format (live POV, live crowd, studio/DAW, face to camera, skit/meme, art/visual, flyer/promo, proof/co-sign, split screen, other), the on-screen text hook word for word, the setting, the views, and whether it's a breakout for that account.
Then analyse **within each artist** (compare posts to that artist's own median, never raw views across artists):
- Which formats over-index among breakouts, and which sit at the floor.
- Which text hooks appear on breakouts vs weakest posts.
- Posting rhythm: average days between posts for consistent vs inconsistent accounts.
- Collabs, famous-song flips/remixes, series, signature looks, platforms (e.g. studio sessions) behind top posts.
Read the lower-ranked accounts too: they show what does NOT carry.

# Step 4: Format / template accounts (optional, ask me first)

Find them through TikTok video search for lane terms and through accounts that repost or collab with the artists above. Reach-read them the same way. Separate consistent pages (most of the last 12 over the threshold) from lottery pages (one huge pinned hit, recent posts tiny). Record each template precisely enough to rebuild.

# What to test against (starting hypotheses, not conclusions)
These held up in a previous study of mid-tier bass artists. Check whether they hold in my lane; report where they don't.
1. **Rhythm is the biggest separator.** Consistent accounts post far more often; follower counts barely differ.
2. **Live footage and memes/skits spike; art, photos and flyers sink.** Promo is usually the floor, unless it's styled like the rest of the feed.
3. **A text hook is an account style, not a per-post lever.** Within one account, posts with and without a hook do about the same; what matters is one consistent voice.
4. **Famous-song flips are the biggest outliers only with a second hook** (a joke, a reaction, a story, a crowd moment). "[song] but make it [genre]" alone usually sits at median.
5. **Collabs carry reach** to non-followers.
6. **Views per follower is roughly flat across tiers;** bigger artists get more views because they have more followers. Growth comes from posts that travel beyond followers.
7. **On TikTok, template accounts can break the follower ceiling,** but most are lottery tickets; the consistent ones post on rhythm and give the viewer a reason to come back ("Artist ID", "Designed For Edits", "headphones on").
8. **What makes a text hook work:** one specific, real detail; a situation, not a description of the music; something recognisable (a song, a place, an in-joke); an open loop the clip pays off; contrast; an identity people sort into; something to do or use (comment, headphones, edit audio). What fails: hype, rhetorical questions, teasers, "out now", day counters, describing the music's quality.
