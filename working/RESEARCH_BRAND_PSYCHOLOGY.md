## §1 — THE HEADLINE: THE EVIDENCE BASE DOES NOT REACH THIS SCALE

**And its own authors say so.** This is the most important finding in the round.

### The 2026 admission ✅

> "Tiny brands – those with <1 % market share – have been overlooked in marketing research. Consequently, we know little about their brand performance metrics or growth patterns."

Barker-Trowse, Dunn, Graham, **Sharp** & Corsi (2026), *Tiny brands, big challenges: The limits of loyalty and the role of penetration in driving growth*, **Journal of Business Research** 204. DOI 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115864
`https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v204y2026ics0148296325006873.html`

**Byron Sharp is a co-author.** The most rigorous camp in marketing, stating in a 2026 peer-reviewed paper that brands under 1% share had been *overlooked* and that *we know little*. Everything published before it about what small brands should do was extrapolation.

**What it found:** even stable tiny brands show *"generally low levels of loyalty, often lower than expected (deficit loyalty)"*; when they grow, gains come *"primarily from increased penetration rather than purchase frequency"*; this *"challenges the idea that tiny brands depend on niche loyalty strategies."*

**What it does NOT cover** ❓ — a brand under 1% of a supermarket category still has distribution, thousands of buyers, and panel measurement. Full text was unreachable (ScienceDirect 403), so the sample floor is unknown. **Do not claim this paper covers a 22-listener artist. It does not say that.**

### Sharp's own boundary conditions ✅ PRIMARY — verified verbatim from the PDF

Sharp, Riebe, Dawes & Danenberg (2002), *A Marketing Economy of Scale*, **Marketing Bulletin** 13, Research Note 1. `http://www.byronsharp.com/resources/7273.pdf`

> "This statistical selection effect must occur when markets are stationary and unpartitioned."

> "without stationarity the Double Jeopardy pattern need not occur."

> "a brand could be differentiated to the extent that it formed a partition of one, for example, the classic niche brand where it had higher loyalty than other brands of similar market share."

**Double jeopardy is a property of markets in equilibrium.** A brand growing from zero is definitionally not in one. And Sharp explicitly allows the niche brand as *"a partition of one"* — the case his followers claim doesn't exist, he names as structurally possible. His own defence: *"Most markets are stationary, or very near to it, most of the time."*

### The strongest published critique attacks the same seam ✅

Formisano, **Pauwels** & Zarantonello (2019), *A broader view on brands' growth and decline*, **International Journal of Market Research**. `https://marketingandmetrics.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/53.-Broader-view.pdf`

> "brands grow the most in non-stationary environments (Trinh & Anesbury, 2015)"

> "EB inferences on growth are derived from a model that assumes stationary markets, that is, markets in equilibrium."

Aggregate stationarity is compatible with individual-brand non-stationarity — and individual-brand growth is what a marketer is trying to cause. They also state a testable absence: *"the EB corpus of studies does not offer experimental designs or longitudinal analyses that investigate causal relationships between perceptions and behaviors."*

⚠ **Recorded honestly because it cuts against the caution above:** they *concede* that *"The smaller the brand, however, the less frequent the purchase and the more the availability strategy finds support in the Dirichlet law."* They think availability logic fits small brands **better**, not worse.

### Personal-brand research exists and is the wrong literature ✅

- Scheidt, Gelhard & Henseler (2020), *Frontiers in Psychology*, 518 articles 1969–2019: *"A comprehensive personal branding framework, let alone a sustainable theory, is yet sorely missing."*
- Bonsu & Anim-Wright (2024), PRISMA screen 897 → 39. Four themes: skills, strategy, social media, **politics**. The literature is about employability, CEOs and politicians — not an artist acquiring an audience.

### MY READ — §1

**The gap between the two literatures is where Wobar actually lives, and it is empty.** Brand-growth research is about supermarket shelves; personal-branding research is about getting hired and getting elected.

That is the permission structure, not a dead end. The authority available is **methodological** — *"I read the papers, I know where the boundary conditions are, I won't sell you archetypes"* — not **findings** authority — *"science says do X."* It is also the honest answer to *"why should I listen to someone with 22 listeners"*: **nobody has data on people at this stage, including the professors.**

**What must never be said:** that "penetration over loyalty" is an established instruction for an artist at this scale. That crosses two boundary conditions at once (scale, stationarity) plus a category jump (FMCG → individual creative persons). It collapses the moment a knowledgeable person pushes.

## §3 — ARCHETYPES: BOTH THE AGENCY CLAIM AND THE DISMISSAL ARE WRONG

This brief was expected to confirm the prior (*archetypes are weakly supported*). It didn't — it's the one brief that changed a fact.

### The empirical test almost nobody cites ✅

Spangenberg, Katie (2021), *Brand Archetypes*, PhD dissertation, University of Washington. `https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/c15713ba-a19c-4da6-9ca8-2758d42560e1`

- The twelve **are** perceptually distinct — 44 of 66 pairs significantly dissimilar ✅
- **The four-motivation grouping fails** — MDS returns clusters on arousal and sphere-of-influence instead ❌
- **18 of 30 real brands read as multiple archetypes** ❌
- **But** archetype match *does* predict cause-brand fit and brand-extension success (Chapters 2–3)

So *"it's all nonsense"* is as wrong as the strong claim.

### The number twelve did not come from Jung ✅

Jung never enumerated twelve; he held archetypes to be effectively unbounded, and where he named a core set it was small (Persona, Shadow, Anima/Animus, Self).

**The chain:** Jung (open-ended) → Campbell (narrative structure, not taxonomy) → **Pearson 1986, *The Hero Within* — six** → **Pearson 1991, *Awakening the Heroes Within* — twelve**, in a personal-development book → Mark & Pearson 2001, twelve **renamed** and grouped into four motivational triads.

Pearson's 1991 twelve were *Innocent, Orphan, Warrior, Caregiver, Seeker, Destroyer, Lover, Creator, Ruler, Magician, Sage, Fool* — Orphan→Everyman, Warrior→Hero, Seeker→Explorer, Destroyer→Outlaw, Fool→Jester. **The twelve enter at step four, from a self-help book, with no derivation from Jung at all.**

### The "97%" on every agency page ❌

The book's empirical claim rests on **one proprietary Young & Rubicam BrandAsset Valuator analysis**: ~50 brands, 1993–1999, split by whether the nearest secondary archetype sat 10%+ below the primary.

> "The MVA of those brands strongly aligned with a single archetype rose by 97% more than the MVA of confused brands."

One study, unreplicated, proprietary, ~50 brands, and a 10% threshold that Spangenberg's multi-archetype finding makes look like an artefact.

### What the blogs destroy ✅

Each archetype in the source has a **six-slot profile: Desire, Goal, Fear, Strategy, Trap, Gift.** Blogs keep Goal, sometimes Fear, and drop the rest. **The Trap is the part that makes it usable** — it names each archetype's failure mode. The book's own unit is the *motivation* with three strategies under it, not a flat list of twelve nouns; the familiar 2×2 "archetype wheel" is a later rationalisation, not the source.

### The defensible weaker version: available to state, not available to borrow ❓

The coordination defence — *archetypes as a generative constraint with no predictive claim* — is stated crisply by practitioners and **never in peer-reviewed work** (control passed: the academic space is populated — Spangenberg 2021, Xara-Brasil 2018, Merlo 2022 — so this is a real gap in the argument, not in the searching).

- **Nick Liddell** states it best: *"The strength of archetype theory doesn't lie in its rigour or its irrefutability but in its ability to focus people on a narrow set of options"* — **and then rejects the framework outright**: *"I'd avoid it if you possibly can."*
- **Rob Meyerson** (The Drum) grants a licence, not a defence: *"If you find brand archetypes useful, that's great, keep using them"* — *"just don't claim it's 'backed by science.'"*
- **Margaret Hartwell**'s *Archetypes in Branding* ships **sixty** cards, not twelve — an implicit admission that the taxonomy is arbitrary and the value is in the prompting.

**The source authors do not hold the weaker version.** Pearson's own site frames archetypes as causal psychological forces.

### The best original beat available ✅

Acar, Tarakci & van Knippenberg (2019), *Journal of Management* 45(1) — 145 empirical studies: moderate constraints help creativity, extreme ones stifle it. **It says nothing about archetypes** — applying it is an inference and must be flagged as one.

But it carries the useful asymmetry: **externally imposed constraints hurt novelty relative to the same constraint self-imposed.** An archetype handed down by a strategist is external. One an artist picks for themselves is self-imposed. **That inverts the entire consultant pitch**, in which the archetype is handed to you.

⚠ **The weaker version has an escape-hatch problem:** *"it's just a generative tool with no predictive claim"* is unfalsifiable in exactly the way the strong version is. Say it first, before a critic does. The constraint literature partially rescues it by specifying conditions under which it would fail.

## §4 — THE EXEMPLAR MAP

21 artists mapped, four fields each (strategy · distinctive assets · when established · what documentation exists). **~65–70% of substantive claims backed by a loaded page**; the rest explicitly marked unverified rather than laundered.

### Shortlist, weighted on transfer to a no-budget artist

1. **Overmono** — two Dobermanns replaced two faces, **installed in 2020, not at the start**, covered by Creative Review. Costs nothing, unmistakable, and shows an identity being *installed* rather than admired finished.
2. **Bicep** — the only act on record admitting v1 was broken: *"It was quite sloppy. Lots of cues were missed."* One collaborator found at a gig and kept for years.
3. **SBTRKT** — the fullest arc: named mask designer who talks → a decade of use → removal, with the artist explaining the cost.
4. **Getter** — the failure teardown nobody is making.
5. **Of The Trees** — thin docs, but the only in-lane method statement (art commissioned before the music, own label).

### Traps ❌

**Daft Punk** (deepest docs, zero transfer, saturated) · **Burial** (everyone knows the strategy, nothing exists on how it was built — a teardown would be speculation dressed as insight) · **Rezz** (most transferable concept, weakest documentation relative to fame — the exact profile of a video made on vibes) · **Malaa / Claptone** (Claptone's in-character interviews actively prevent analysis) · **CharlesTheFirst** (nothing to work with, and a deceased artist crosses the testimony line).

### The failures section is the richest untouched material

- **Getter** ✅ strongest case — *Visceral* (2018) moved from heavy dubstep to introspective material. Booed, objects thrown at him (Stereo Live, Houston), remainder of tour cancelled. *"the constant hate and the disgusting attitudes I'm faced with are destroying me."* Project ended December 2024. **Identity is a contract; the danger of breaking it is proportional to how specific the original promise was.**
- **SBTRKT** ✅ best-documented backfire — removed the mask in 2023: *"having a mask or an anonymous persona was only hindering"* ownership of his identity, and the sharpest line in the map: ***"A lot of people thought I was voiceless."*** As a mixed-race South Asian musician, the mask meant to foreground the music erased his authorship. **Anonymity does not create a neutral vacuum — the audience fills it, often with something worse than the truth.**
- **deadmau5** — trapped legally (Disney trademark opposition, 2014) and physically (spinal X-ray captioned *"'Wear a mouse head' they said. 'It'll be fun!' They said"*). **A costume identity is a recurring cost, not a one-time build.**
- **Burial** — unmasked on a one-day margin, self-outing on MySpace 5 Aug 2008 ahead of *The Sun*'s piece. **You don't control the timing; only whether you built something that survives it.**
- **Daft Punk** — a persona with no exit ramp becomes a cage. **Design the ending when you design the beginning.**
- **Fred again..** ⚠ **the conditional trap and the one closest to Wobar's own posture** — documentation-as-identity attacked as manufactured authenticity. His team describing the 2022 Boiler Room as wanting *"to create virality"* is the smoking gun. **If the core claim is "this is my real life," any evidence of staging invalidates the whole proposition.**

### Fabrication risks the agent caught in its own material ⚠

The "$55,000 Marshmello helmet" figure is **not** in Forbes · the "$65,000 Daft Punk helmets" figure came only from a Threads post · `rezzstudios.com` is an unrelated design agency, not the artist. **Do not reuse these.**

### The structural observation worth keeping ✅

The map splits into **artists who bought an identity** (Daft Punk, Marshmello, Floating Points, Prydz) and **artists who chose one repeated cheap thing and never stopped** (Overmono's dogs, Four Tet's photographer, Aphex's hand-drawn A, Rezz's goggles, Of The Trees' cover-first method). **The second group is the entire content series. The first group is a foil.**

### And the open field ✅

**No analysis of the US bass / dark-140 lane's identity practices exists at all.** Of The Trees, G Jones, CharlesTheFirst, Tipper — nothing beyond craft interviews and tour reviews. Meanwhile the Aphex logo and Daft Punk branding are taken ground.

## §5 — THE CONVERGENCE

Four briefs, run independently, point at the same conclusion — and it is **not** the series as planned.

| Brief | What it says about the prescriptive posture |
|---|---|
| 1 — archetypes | The defence for teaching archetypes would have to be **invented**, not borrowed. Nobody credible argues it in print. |

| 5 — landscape | The education format was **run properly and died** at 150–700 views. The audience is hostile to uncredentialed strategy teaching. The one working format is **criticism**. |

## §6 — THE COLLISION WITH THE ROADMAP

**Flagged, not resolved.** [[working/WOBAR_ROADMAP]] §1 stakes the goal on return over reach: *"12 months: people who come back"*, fan ≠ follower, and §1.2 **duration not reach**.

The best available evidence on the small end points the other way. The 2026 *Tiny brands* paper finds brands under 1% share show **deficit** loyalty — less than double jeopardy predicts, not more — and that when they grow, gains come *primarily from penetration rather than purchase frequency*. It explicitly *"challenges the idea that tiny brands depend on niche loyalty strategies."*

**This does not overturn the goal, and must not be reported as if it does:**

- The paper measures distributed FMCG brands in panel data. Its floor is unknown (full text unreachable). It does not reach 22 monthly listeners.
- Sharp's own stationarity condition means a brand growing from zero sits outside the law's stated scope in the first place.
- Formisano et al. **concede** availability logic fits small brands *better*, which cuts against reading this as a caution.
- The standing trap applies directly: **borrowed numbers read as first-party results.** Doing to the roadmap what the flat comp list did to the EPK would be the same error in a new domain.

**What is genuinely open:** the roadmap currently carries no note that the best evidence on small brands points away from a loyalty-first bet. Whether that changes the goal, qualifies it, or is correctly dismissed as out-of-scope is Nick's call and has not been made.

## §8 — METHOD NOTES WORTH CARRYING FORWARD

1. **The control rule works and earned its keep again.** Brief 5's control passed loudly and *falsified* the plan's own expectation. Brief 1's control converted "no peer-reviewed defence exists" from a guess into a supportable claim by proving the academic space returns results. §9 of [[working/RESEARCH_STORYTELLING]] holds.
2. **A pre-loaded absence in the brief is as dangerous as a scraped zero.** New failure mode, same class. The prompt itself can manufacture the false finding. **Never write "almost certainly not — confirm with evidence" into a brief.**
3. **Vocabulary mismatch produces false absences.** `brand psychology` returns nothing; `artist branding` returns the whole field. Before reporting scarcity, vary the vocabulary aggressively — the practitioners in a space rarely use the analyst's word for it.
4. **YouTube's `ytInitialData` is reachable via plain curl with a desktop UA.** Free, unlimited, primary enumeration of channels, subscriber counts, view counts and chapter lists — no WebSearch budget consumed. This is how the decisive finding was obtained. Same class of tool as `scripts/reddit_research.py`.
5. **Marketing blogs recycle each other — five blogs agreeing is one source.** This topic is the most commercially polluted the vault has researched. The archetype layer in particular is agency lead-magnet material almost end to end.
6. **Distinguish the principals from the followers.** Sharp's written position is consistently more hedged than his advocates' version, and his critics frequently attack the advocate version. Both camps' principals state their own boundary conditions; the internet fight does not.
